Deep Learning Architectures for Network Intrusion and Anomaly Detection
IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, 2024
Network intrusion detection systems require robust feature representation to distinguish legitimate traffic from malicious exploitation attempts. Deep neural networks, including recurrent neural networks and long short-term memory networks, effectively capture temporal dependencies within raw packet streams and connection logs. Convolutional neural networks applied to packet header matrices extract spatial spatial correlations that bypass human-engineered heuristics. In benchmark evaluations on the NSL-KDD and CICIDS-2017 datasets, deep learning models demonstrate high detection accuracy and low false alarm rates across distributed denial of service, brute force, and port scanning attacks. Furthermore, autoencoders trained strictly on benign traffic establish a reconstruction baseline; abnormal packets yielding high reconstruction error trigger immediate intrusion alerts. The computational cost of deep inference is mitigated via quantized neural architectures deployed on edge security gateways. This enables real-time packet inspection at multi-gigabit throughput.

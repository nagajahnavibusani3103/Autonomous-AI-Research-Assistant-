Adversarial Attacks on Machine Learning Models in Network Security
IEEE Security & Privacy, 2024
While machine learning enhances automated threat detection, security classifiers are inherently susceptible to adversarial manipulation. Attackers employ evasion attacks during test time, perturbing malicious inputs with imperceptible modifications that trigger misclassification into benign classes. In network intrusion detection, evasion involves packet padding and timing jitter to disguise command-and-control communication. Conversely, poisoning attacks target the training phase by injecting contaminated telemetry into continuous learning pipelines, creating backdoors that blind the model to specific exploitation vectors. Developing adversarial robustness requires defensive distillation, adversarial training with perturbation bounds, and certified robustness guarantees. Security architects must incorporate explainability mechanisms to audit decision boundaries and verify model integrity against deliberate evasion.

Natural Language Processing for Automated Research Synthesis and Fact Extraction
Knowledge-Based Systems, 2024
The exponential growth of scientific literature demands autonomous systems capable of extracting, organizing, and synthesizing domain facts from unstructured publications. Automated research synthesis pipelines decompose overarching inquiries into targeted sub-hypotheses, execute targeted retrieval over local inverted indices, and aggregate supporting evidence. Unlike generative language models that risk fabricating citations and hallucinating non-existent studies, extractive synthesis maintains strict provenance by anchoring every finding to verbatim document passages. Sentence-level relevance scoring filters out ancillary exposition, while similarity-based deduplication eliminates redundant claims across multi-document sets. Traceable citation mapping ensures verifiable lineage, enabling human researchers to audit claims against peer-reviewed literature with complete transparency.

Vector Space Models and Inverted Indexing in Information Retrieval Systems
ACM Computing Surveys, Vol. 56, 2024
The inverted index is the fundamental data structure powering modern text retrieval systems and web search engines. In contrast to forward indexing, which maps documents to the terms they contain, an inverted index maps each unique vocabulary term to a postings list of documents containing that term, alongside term frequency and positional occurrences. In vector space models, documents and queries are represented as high-dimensional sparse vectors where dimensions correspond to vocabulary terms. Document retrieval is transformed into an efficient sparse dot product calculation, evaluating cosine similarity between the query vector and candidate document vectors. By maintaining posting lists sorted by document identifier or term impact, retrieval engines avoid scanning non-matching documents, pruning candidate sets through early termination algorithms like WAND. Memory-efficient posting list compression techniques, such as variable-byte and bit-packing encoding, significantly reduce the RAM footprint of multi-million document collections.

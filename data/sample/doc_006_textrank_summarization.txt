Extractive Text Summarization Using Graph-Based TextRank Algorithms
Computational Linguistics and Language Technologies, 2024
Extractive text summarization distills comprehensive scientific manuscripts into concise briefs by identifying and extracting the most informative, central sentences without modifying the source prose. Graph-based ranking models, notably TextRank, adapt Google's PageRank algorithm to Natural Language Processing tasks. In this formulation, individual sentences serve as vertices in a weighted, undirected or directed graph, and edge weights represent lexical semantic similarity between sentence pairs, typically calculated via token overlap normalized by sentence length or vector cosine distance. An iterative random walk process is executed until vertex saliency scores converge below a predefined tolerance threshold. High-scoring sentences correspond to central discourse hubs that exhibit strong semantic connectivity across the manuscript. By selecting top-ranked sentences and ordering them according to their original narrative chronology, TextRank preserves factual coherence and eliminates artificial hallucinations characteristic of abstractive neural models.
